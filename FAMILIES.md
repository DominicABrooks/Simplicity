# The Families of Kingsholm

Who's who in the capital: where every named family came from, who they love, owe, feud with and spy on,
and what the city says about them behind their backs. The same families, with their members, ages,
houses and customs, are in [`src/world/people.ts`](src/world/people.ts). The kingdom's history is in
[HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md), and the city as it is now is in [LORE.md](LORE.md).

Rumours are marked **(true)**, **(false)** or **(half true)**. The city doesn't know which is which.

## The web

The threads that tie the city together, at a glance.

### Feuds and rivalries

- **The Stotts and the Cleaves**, butchers, three doors apart. Old Wilfred Stott left his shop "to the
  better butcher" in 560 AF, and his children sawed it in half. Forty years of undercutting, spite
  sausages and not saying hello.
- **The Crown and the Bell**, the two inns facing each other across Castle Street since 355 AF. The Crown
  is where the city talks; the Bell is where the castle drinks. Each claims the other waters its cider.
- **Father Gale and Dean Thorne.** Gale wants the Dean's job and tells the Marshal things to help it
  along. Thorne knows, and keeps Gale counting coins so he's too busy to do anything worse.
- **The Marshal and Old Mother Larke.** He's sure she's a witch. She's sure he's a coward. Lady Harrow
  buys Ivy's headache tea every Tuesday by the back door, and neither of them has told him.

### Love and matches

- **Hal Stott and Marian Cleave**, the feud's two youngest, meet behind St Giles' chapel every Sunday
  after the service. Half the Market Quarter knows. Neither of their fathers does.
- **Piers Harrow and Joan Leafwright** are engaged: a marriage between the Marshal's sword and the
  crown's goldbeaters. Piers doesn't want it. Joan wants it even less, because she has her eye on Benet
  Quill's handwriting.
- **Hugh Fitzwaren and Alys Ashcombe** are being pushed together by their mothers. Lady Cecily wants the
  Ashcombe money. Philippa Ashcombe wants the Fitzwaren name. Both families are broke, and neither knows
  the other is.
- **Cuthbert Pike and Gwen Maddox.** The Bell's son drinks at the Crown "because it's warm". It's
  because of Gwen's pies. It's also because of Gwen.
- **Prince Edmund and Lucia Grison.** The heir meets the moneylender's daughter in the guildhall's
  records room to go over the crown's debts, because nobody else will tell him the truth about them.
  It's strictly business. Lucia has started wearing her good earrings to it.

### Debts

- **The crown owes the Grisons** for the King's Bridge: about 200,000 crowns, secured on the southern
  road's trade, which has stopped.
- **The Ashcombes owe the Grisons** 4,000 crowns, and **the crown owes the Ashcombes** 6,000 for cloth
  for the Gilding.
- **Hugh Fitzwaren owes the Grisons** 900 crowns in dice debts, which his mother thinks is 90.
- **The Wallside owes the Hollins** for about two years of bread, which Joan Hollin writes in a book
  marked *paid*.

### Secrets kept together

- **The Crown's cellar.** It runs under the square to the guildhall. Bethan Maddox, Meg Fell and Simon
  Quill have keys. Quill keeps his private record of the Edict down there, and Meg keeps everything
  Wystan ever wrote.
- **The falcon post.** Wystan Fell's cell in the keep has a grating high up in the wall. Elinor Hawke's
  old goshawk, **Ethelind**, flies to it every few nights with notes from Meg and back with notes from
  Wystan. The whistle Wren hears at night is real.
- **The chronicle.** The King, Father Pryce and (since a long night in 598 AF) Master Hugh Kellet know
  what Aldric really did. Pryce has hidden a copy where the Fells will find it if he dies.
- **The chimney.** Dick Grubb sweeps the keep's chimneys. He has heard the King talking to the Ranger at
  night, and he has told exactly one person: Bethan Maddox.

### Friendships

- **Dowager Queen Hild and Widow Crane** play cards at the almshouse every Thursday. Hild arrives in a
  plain cloak and pretends nobody recognises her. Everybody does. Crane cheats.
- **Princess Rosamund and Blanche Fitzwaren**, best friends. When Rosamund goes to the Crown, Blanche
  tells the castle she's at the Fitzwarens'. Pip Tansy carries the lantern.
- **Wren Fell and Robin Linnet**, nine and eight, the two children whose fathers are in the dungeon. They
  sit on the wall over Southgate every evening and wave at the keep.
- **Hob Fell and Jory Rudd** are planning to row up the Aurel and walk south through the Witchwood to
  find out what Hob's father saw. Jory's mother has found the bag they've packed, and is pretending she
  hasn't.
- **Tibby Waxley and Isabel Kellet** explore the crypt of St Mary's whenever the masons aren't looking.
  They've been through the little carved door. They've told no one.

## The castle

### House Aurel, the royal family

*Kings of Aurenmark since the Founding.*

**Origins.** Descended from Aldric the Founder through twenty kings and queens (see the
[king list](HISTORY.md#the-kings-and-queens-of-aurenmark)). Every one of them has gone very red on
reading the true chronicle, and every one but Matilda the Bold decided to say nothing about it.

**Today.** **Osric IV**, the Gilded, has spent forty years making Aurenmark shine, and the last one
trying to stop anyone noticing the shine is borrowed. He's not wicked, just frightened and proud, and he
would rather the whole city whispered than admit he was wrong. **Queen Isolde**, from the fishing coast,
married him to end the Salt War's last grudges and has quietly become the castle's real manager. **Prince
Edmund**, the heir, is a widower and captain of the Royal Guard. He held the Ranger's cold orb and
believes every word, and has been quietly feeding the Ranger's mule and visiting Meg Fell. **Princess
Rosamund** is clever, bored and spends her evenings at the Crown in a laundress's hood. **Dowager Queen
Hild**, of the Hills, is half blind, sharp as a pin, and the only person in the castle who says what
everyone's thinking. **Little Prince Aldric**, six, dreams every night of a great bird singing to him.

**Ties.** Owes the Grisons for the bridge. Commands the Harrows. Protects the Pikes' tattling. Edmund and
Lucia Grison, Rosamund and Blanche Fitzwaren, Hild and Widow Crane.

**Rumours.**
- The King goes down to the dungeon at night with cake. **(true)** Lemon cake. The Ranger prefers seed
  cake and hasn't had the heart to say.
- The Queen is a Vael spy from the coast. **(false)** She's from a fishing family and gets seasick.
- Prince Edmund is planning to free the Ranger. **(half true)** He's planning to *ask* his father to.
  He's been planning it for three months.
- Little Prince Aldric talks in his sleep in a language nobody knows. **(true)** It's Vael. It's a
  lullaby.

### The Harrows

*Marshals of Aurenmark, lords since the Salt War.*

**Origins.** In 263 AF **Sergeant Gerald Harrow** held the old timber bridge for three days with a
pushcart and four friends, and Queen Hilda made him a lord and gave him the crimson door. Every Harrow
since has been a soldier. The pushcart is kept in the family hall, painted gold.

**Today.** **Lord Gerard Harrow**, the Marshal, enforces the Edict with a list, a frown and absolute
certainty that a panicking city is worse than any witch. Underneath, he's terrified of the dark and
sleeps with three candles. **Lady Ysolde** gathers gossip at Lady Cecily's table for him, and buys
headache tea from Old Mother Larke behind his back. **Bran**, the elder son, sergeant of the bridge
watch, has seen a robed figure walk across the King's Bridge at midnight, bow politely to him and
vanish, and wrote it down as "a pilgrim". **Piers**, the younger, is engaged to Joan Leafwright and thinks
his father's a coward for hiding behind an edict.

**Ties.** Pays Gilbert Pike and Father Gale for names. Commands Tom Bowyer and the bridge watch (where
Will Bowyer serves under Bran). Orders arrowheads from the Hammonds. Piers and Joan Leafwright.

**Rumours.**
- The Marshal's list has the whole Crown on it. **(half true)** It has Bethan Maddox on it. He can't bring
  himself to arrest her because she does the best pie in the city.
- The golden pushcart is cursed. **(false)** It squeaks.
- Bran Harrow has seen a witch on the bridge. **(true)**
- The Harrows' steward tattles on them too. **(true)** About the candles.

### The Fitzwarens

*Courtiers, lords of the southern manors.*

**Origins.** One of Aldric's eleven friends was a Fitzwaren, and the family has held the farmland on the
Witchwood's edge ever since. Fitzwaren manors grow the best apples in the kingdom, and the family wears
green on Gilding Day because their first lord was colour-blind and nobody liked to tell him.

**Today.** **Lady Cecily**, a widow, keeps the best supper table in the city on the first of every month,
where courtiers say too much and Lady Harrow writes it down. Her manors have sent no rents since spring,
and the reeves have stopped writing. **Hugh** plays dice at the Crown and loses. **Blanche** is Princess
Rosamund's best friend and her alibi.

**Ties.** Owes the Grisons (Hugh). Is trying to marry Hugh to Alys Ashcombe. Daisy Tansy sells posies at
their gate and hears everything.

**Rumours.**
- The southern manors have been taken by witches. **(half true)** The Vael are camping in the orchards.
  They're picking the apples and leaving payment in strange silver coins in the reeve's hat.
- Lady Cecily has sold the family silver. **(true)** She's replaced it with painted tin. Nobody at her
  table has noticed yet.

### The Hammonds

*Royal armourers since the Founder's grandson.*

**Origins.** The first Hammond made Aldric II's sword, a very serious sword for a very serious king. The
family forge has stood by the castle ever since, and every Hammond baby's first toy is a little iron
hammer.

**Today.** **Walter Hammond** has orders for twelve thousand arrowheads "for a war that isn't coming",
and business has never been better, which worries him more than anything. **Agnes** keeps the accounts
and the peace. **Kit** is a decent journeyman. **Nell**, fifteen, is a far better smith than her
brother, and everyone knows and nobody's allowed to say. She's secretly making Prince Edmund a sword that
stays cold to the touch, quenched in water from the melted frost on the Ranger's orb.

**Ties.** Supplies the Harrows and the Royal Guard. Nell and Prince Edmund. Buys charcoal from the
Grubbs.

**Rumours.**
- The arrowheads are for a war with the coast. **(false)** The coast has more fish than arrows.
- Nell Hammond can forge with her bare hands. **(half true)** She can hold hot iron a moment longer than
  anyone should be able to. Old Mother Larke has had a look at her eyes and said nothing.

### The Hawkes

*Royal falconers.*

**Origins.** Falconers since Queen Matilda, who kept a white gyrfalcon that was said to understand
speech. Every Hawke falcon is named after a king or queen, so the loft currently holds three Osrics, two
Edgars and a very bad-tempered Hilda.

**Today.** **Osmund Hawke**, the master falconer, is going deaf and pretends he isn't. **Elinor**, his
daughter, flies the birds now. Her best peregrine came back from the south with singed wing feathers and
won't fly that way again. **Robin**, eleven, wants to be a falconer more than anything.

**Ties.** Elinor's old goshawk Ethelind carries notes between Meg Fell and the Ranger's cell. The Hawkes
flew every bird in the loft for Prince Edmund's birth.

**Rumours.**
- A Hawke falcon has seen the dragon. **(true)** That's what singed it.
- Elinor Hawke talks to her birds and they answer. **(false)** She talks to them. They ignore her, like
  all falcons.

### The Garths

*Keepers of the castle storehouse.*

**Origins.** Grooms, carters and porters as far back as the castle, which is to say they've carried
everything into it that's ever been in it. The castle stable was pulled down this year to make room for
a stone storehouse, and the Garths went from mucking out horses to counting sacks.

**Today.** **Daw Garth** keeps the storehouse and knows exactly how much flour the castle has (enough for
a siege, which is new). **Moll** does the castle laundry, which means she knows whose sheets have frost on
them in the morning. Their boys **Sim**, **Tib** and **Little Daw** run errands. Sim looks after the only
animal left in the castle yard: **Biscuit**, the mule the Vael lent the Ranger, kept on Prince Edmund's
orders. Biscuit won't eat hay. Biscuit eats turnips, snow and, once, a guard's hat.

**Ties.** Sim and Prince Edmund (who visits Biscuit). Moll and the Pikes' laundry. Daw orders flour from
the Hollins.

**Rumours.**
- The storehouse is full of weapons. **(false)** It's full of flour. A *lot* of flour.
- Biscuit the mule can understand Vael. **(true)** She also understands "turnip" in every language.

## The Market Quarter

### The Maddoxes

*Keepers of the Crown inn since 352 AF.*

**Origins.** **Morwen Maddox** opened the Crown in 352 AF "so that everyone can say they drank under the
Crown", and every landlord since has been a Maddox woman. The back room's rule, *what's said under the
Crown stays under the Crown*, is carved over its door.

**Today.** **Bethan**, a widow, decides who sits in the back room after the curfew bell. No one has ever
been taken from it, because the Marshal can't bring himself to give up her pies. **Owain** runs the bar.
**Gwen** makes the pies. **Lew**, the pot-boy, knows every word of Jack Linnet's song and taught most of
them to Princess Rosamund.

**Ties.** Feud with the Pikes. Keys to the cellar shared with Meg Fell and Simon Quill. Gwen and Cuthbert
Pike. The Grubbs drink free. Rosamund drinks in disguise.

**Rumours.**
- The Crown's cellar connects to the castle. **(false)** It connects to the guildhall, which is almost as
  good.
- Bethan Maddox knows where the Ranger is. **(true)** Dick Grubb told her.
- The Crown's cider is watered. **(false)** That's the Bell's.

### The Pikes

*Keepers of the Bell inn since 412 AF.*

**Origins.** The Bell opened in 355 AF across Castle Street from the Crown, "so that everyone can hear
when the Crown runs dry". The Pikes bought it in 412 AF and have spent two centuries losing customers to
the Crown and winning them from the castle.

**Today.** **Gilbert Pike** tattles everything he overhears to the Marshal at a shilling a name, and
tells himself it's loyalty. **Maud** puts the shillings in the poor box at St Giles' and prays for him.
**Cuthbert**, seventeen, drinks at the Crown for Gwen Maddox's pies and Gwen Maddox. He's started warning
people when his father's listening.

**Ties.** Informs for the Harrows. Feud with the Maddoxes. Cuthbert and Gwen. Blue sign glazed by the
Dunns.

**Rumours.**
- Gilbert Pike is a tattletale. **(true)** Everyone knows. That's why the Bell is so quiet.
- Cuthbert Pike is the one warning people. **(true)** By whistling the Linnet song when his father comes
  in.
- The Bell's portrait of the King is regilded every month. **(true)** By the Leafwrights, on credit.

### The Ashcombes

*Mercers, masters of the cloth trade.*

**Origins.** The first Ashcombe signed the Mercers' Charter in 226 AF, the guild's right to trade on the
southern road. For most of the family's history they were comfortable. Three generations ago, Aldous's
grandfather opened a cloth route through the Witchwood to the dye-towns of the south, and they became
the richest commoners in the city.

**Today.** **Aldous Ashcombe** is master of the Mercers' Guild, owes the Grisons 4,000 crowns, is owed
6,000 by the crown, and smiles very hard. **Philippa** has sewn the family rings into her winter cloaks
just in case. **Thomas**, the eldest, was sent "to the cousins in the north" with the silver. **Alys**
is being steered toward Hugh Fitzwaren, **Margery** writes poetry about it, and **Kate**, thirteen,
thinks the whole thing is hilarious.

**Ties.** Debts with the Grisons and the crown. The Fitzwaren match. The Quills drew up the Charter.

**Rumours.**
- The Ashcombes are ruined. **(half true)** On paper they're rich. In coin, they have about a week.
- The southern dyes they sell are made by witches. **(true)** The Vael dye-towns make the best red in the
  world. The Ember order does it with a look.

### The Hollins

*Bakers to the square since the Bakers' Guild was founded.*

**Origins.** Founding members of the Bakers' Guild after the bread riot in Osric I's reign. They insist
the Great Pie Fire of 318 AF was not their pie. (It was their pie.)

**Today.** **Joan Hollin** cut the first three-cut loaf the week of the Edict, and swears it's just a
pattern. **Wat** works the oven. **Peg**, **Ned** and **Bess** deliver bread across the city before
dawn. The first loaf of every day goes to the almshouse, and the Wallside's debts go in a book marked
*paid*.

**Ties.** Feeds the almshouse and the Wallside. Supplies the castle storehouse (all that flour). Bakes
the gold-flake crown loaves with leaf from the Leafwrights.

**Rumours.**
- The three cuts are a witch-mark. **(half true)** They're the Vael signs for Ember, Frost and Storm.
  Joan copied them off a loaf she was given at the Witchwood's edge as a girl and has never told anyone.
- The Hollins started the Great Pie Fire. **(true)**

### The Stotts

*Butchers, the bigger half.*

**Origins.** Old Wilfred Stott's son Ralf got the half of the shop with the counter's good end, in 560
AF. It's been the Stotts' proudest possession ever since.

**Today.** **Roger Stott** gives the Marshal the best cut every Founder's Night and votes loyal on
everything, mainly because the Cleaves don't. **Annis** runs the till. **Hal**, twenty, is in love with
Marian Cleave, and it is a scandal waiting to happen.

**Ties.** Feud with the Cleaves. Hal and Marian. Loyal to the Harrows.

**Rumours.**
- Roger Stott would rather close than sell to a Cleave. **(true)**
- Hal Stott is courting someone. **(true)** His mother thinks it's Daisy Tansy.

### The Cleaves

*Butchers, the other half.*

**Origins.** Wilfred Stott's daughter **Ursula** married **Bartholomew Cleave** and took the half of the
shop with the good hooks. She started the feud and has outlived everyone else who remembers why.

**Today.** **Abel Cleave**, Ursula's son, buys his beasts from the south farms, so he hears the farmers'
stories first. **Marian**, nineteen, meets Hal Stott behind St Giles'. **Old Ursula**, seventy-seven,
sits in the shop window and glares across the street.

**Ties.** Feud with the Stotts. Marian and Hal. Buys from the same south farmers as the Applebys.

**Rumours.**
- Old Ursula started the feud over a sausage. **(true)** She's never said which sausage.
- The drovers won't take the south road now. **(true)** Abel has had to start raising his own pigs in the
  yard.

### The Applebys

*Greengrocers.*

**Origins.** Turnip farmers from the south who came into the city after the hard winter of 512 AF.
They've never lost the habit of eating turnips with everything, including pudding.

**Today.** **Hob Appleby** buys from the farms at the Witchwood's edge, or did until spring. **Sarra**
runs the shop. And there's **Uncle**, a sheep who turned up in their yard the day Hob's nephew **Tam**
went into the Witchwood after a strayed cow. Uncle stares, follows Sarra to church, sits in Tam's chair
and won't eat grass, only turnips.

**Ties.** Have asked Old Mother Larke to turn Uncle back. Sell to the Cleaves' farmers' wives. Sarra and
Moll Garth are cousins.

**Rumours.**
- The sheep is Tam. **(true)** An Ember mage's orb. He'll turn back on his own in a few years, or sooner
  if someone asks an Ember mage nicely.
- The Applebys have gone soft in the head. **(false)** The sheep really does sit in the chair.

### The Dunns

*Potters.*

**Origins.** Potters since the clay pits by the river were first dug, under Aldric II. Their blue glaze is
a family secret, and it's the blue of the Bell's sign.

**Today.** **Gil** and **Edith Dunn** run the kiln. **Aud**, their daughter, is the better potter and
runs the shop. The morning after the first storm of the summer their kiln wouldn't light, and then lit
itself. They haven't used flint on it since.

**Ties.** Made the Bell's sign. Fire a crock for every baby on the street. Sell Old Mother Larke her jars.

**Rumours.**
- The Dunns' kiln is haunted. **(half true)** A little Ember warmth has got into it. It's friendly. It
  likes being thanked.
- The blue glaze is made with ground-up sapphires. **(false)** Blueberries.

### The Leafwrights

*Goldbeaters to the crown since 421 AF.*

**Origins.** Came from the coast in 421 AF, where they beat gold leaf for church altars. The Gilding made
them: every flake of gold on the city came through their hammers.

**Today.** **Ambrose Leafwright** has beaten every leaf on the King's Bridge and is owed for about half
of it. **Helewise** keeps the books and the peace with the Harrows. **Joan**, eighteen, is engaged to
Piers Harrow and in love with Benet Quill's handwriting. **Jasper**, fourteen, is apprenticed and very
loud.

**Ties.** The crown is their only customer (and owes them). Joan and Piers Harrow. Regild the Bell's
portrait. Supply the Hollins' crown-loaf gold.

**Rumours.**
- The Leafwrights are the richest family in the city. **(false)** They're owed the most.
- The gold leaf on the bridge is fake. **(half true)** The leaf on the last forty posts is brass. The King
  ran out of gold, and Ambrose couldn't bear to tell him.

### The Grisons

*Moneylenders, from over the sea.*

**Origins.** Came from over the sea in 436 AF with three chests of coin, and have lent to five kings.
They trust no edict, no promise and no handshake, not even at the market cross.

**Today.** **Benedict Grison** holds the crown's notes for the King's Bridge and can't sleep for them.
**Lucia**, his daughter and partner, is the cleverest person on the square and has been quietly moving
the family's coin to a northern port, one cart a week, under loads of turnips.

**Ties.** Everyone owes them: the crown, the Ashcombes, Hugh Fitzwaren. Lucia and Prince Edmund. Rent the
cart from the Applebys.

**Rumours.**
- The Grisons are leaving. **(true)**
- Lucia Grison is sweet on the Prince. **(true)** She'd rather be thrown in the dungeon than admit it.
- The Grisons have a secret vault under the square. **(false)** It's under the turnips.

### The Quills

*Town clerks and scriveners.*

**Origins.** The first Quill lent the first Ashcombe his quill to sign the Mercers' Charter in 226 AF,
and the family have written down the city's every law, birth, wedding and quarrel since.

**Today.** **Master Simon Quill** reads the Edict at the cross every market day, a little more quietly
each time, and keeps a second, private record of everything the Edict hides, in the Crown's cellar.
**Alice** writes letters for people who can't. **Benet**, sixteen, copies Jack Linnet's song by night
and slips it under doors. His handwriting is beautiful, and Joan Leafwright has noticed.

**Ties.** Cellar keys with the Maddoxes and Meg Fell. Benet and Joan Leafwright. Copying the song with the
Linnets.

**Rumours.**
- Someone's copying Jack Linnet's song and posting it under doors. **(true)** It's Benet.
- Master Quill wrote the Edict. **(false)** The King wrote it. Quill fixed the spelling.

### The Fells

*The Ranger's family.*

**Origins.** Foresters and trackers who've served the crown since Queen Matilda took a Fell with her into
the Witchwood in 64 AF. Fells are the only men in the kingdom who know the old paths through the forest.

**Today.** **Wystan Fell**, the King's Ranger, is in the dungeon under the keep. **Meg**, a laundress,
hangs his grey cloak out of the window every evening, and every few nights a goshawk brings her a note.
**Hob**, fourteen, is planning to go south through the Witchwood with Jory Rudd. **Wren**, nine, hears
her father whistle from the keep at night, and she's right.

**Ties.** Cellar key from the Maddoxes. Falcon post with the Hawkes. Prince Edmund visits. Wren and Robin
Linnet. Hob and Jory Rudd. Father Pryce's hidden copy is meant for them.

**Rumours.**
- The Ranger is dead. **(false)**
- The Ranger is a Vael spy. **(false)** He's a Fell. Fells don't spy, they track.
- Hob Fell is going to run off south. **(true)** On the night of Founder's Night.

### The Larkes

*Herbwives and midwives.*

**Origins.** Herbwives as far back as anyone remembers. **Rhosyn Larke** cured the Fever Winter of 377 AF
with a tea nobody else could make. The truth, which only the Larkes know: their first grandmother was
**Yssa of the Frost**'s great-niece, a Vael girl who stayed behind in the valley in 9 AF because she'd
fallen in love with a turnip farmer.

**Today.** **Old Mother Ivy Larke**, seventy, sells salt and rowan against the weather, and thinks the
city's fuss is the funniest thing that's happened in years. She can light a candle by looking at it.
**Tamsin**, her granddaughter, is learning the herbs and, quietly, the old words.

**Ties.** Feud with the Marshal. Lady Harrow's headache tea. Trying to turn Uncle back for the Applebys.
Old friends with Widow Crane. Leaves the milk bowl at the forest's edge, as Queen Matilda ordered.

**Rumours.**
- Old Mother Larke is a witch. **(half true)** She's about one part in sixteen Vael, and a very good
  herbwife. She has never once turned anyone into anything.
- Tamsin Larke has been seen talking to a robed woman at the Witchwood's edge. **(true)** It's her
  great-great-great-(and so on)-aunt, Yssa of the Frost, who is very old and very fond of her.

### The Linnets

*Singers and players.*

**Origins.** Travelling players who came to Kingsholm for Gilding Day in 590 AF and never left, because
Jack fell in love with the city (and with Kat).

**Today.** **Jack Linnet** is in the castle dungeon for his song about the Ranger's orb. He sings it every
night, and the jailer joins in on the chorus. **Kat** spins wool and keeps the children fed with help from
the whole Wallside. **Robin** and **Wenna** taught every child in the Wallside the song with nonsense words
in place of the real ones.

**Ties.** The Hollins' *paid* book. Benet Quill copies the song. Wren Fell and Robin are best friends.
Daisy Tansy leaves the day's last flower on their step.

**Rumours.**
- Jack Linnet is in the cell next to the Ranger. **(true)** They've written a second verse together.
- The song has a secret verse that tells where the eggs are. **(false)** It has a secret verse about the
  Marshal's candles.

### The Rudds

*River fishers.*

**Origins.** Fishers on the Aurel since before there was a bridge. A Rudd ferried Aldric's eleven friends
across the river in 0 AF, and the family has never been paid for it.

**Today.** **Silas Rudd** saw blue fire walking on the water under the King's Bridge one night in spring.
**Hester** sells the catch in the square and says the fish are all swimming north, early. **Jory**,
seventeen, dives for the Dean's coins at every Bridge Blessing and has never missed one. He's agreed to
row Hob Fell upriver.

**Ties.** Jory and Hob Fell. Sell to the Crown's kitchen. Saw the same thing on the bridge as Bran Harrow.

**Rumours.**
- The Rudds have a hoard of Bridge Blessing coins. **(true)** Seventeen years' worth, in a sock.
- The blue fire on the river was a Frost mage. **(true)** Walking, because the ice holds her up.

### The Grubbs

*Lamplighters and chimney sweeps.*

**Origins.** Sweeps and lamplighters father to son since Osbert the Rebuilder put lamps on the high street
after the Pie Fire. Sound carries up a chimney beautifully, and nobody ever thinks to lower their voice
for the sweep.

**Today.** **Dick Grubb** lights the street lamps at dusk and sweeps chimneys by day, so he's been on
every roof in the city, including the keep's. He's heard the King talking to the Ranger at night, and has
told only Bethan Maddox. **Nan** sells his soot to the gardeners and his gossip to nobody.

**Ties.** Drinks free at the Crown. Sells charcoal to the Hammonds. Knows who Gilbert Pike reports to, and
when.

**Rumours.**
- Dick Grubb knows everyone's secrets. **(true)** He's too polite to use them.
- The Grubbs are allowed out after curfew. **(true)** By ancient right, in a charter signed by Osbert the
  Rebuilder that nobody has ever checked.

### The Bowyers

*Sergeants of Southgate.*

**Origins.** Bowyers who made the bows for the Salt War and became gate guards when the war ended. A
Bowyer has locked Southgate every night for two hundred years.

**Today.** **Tom Bowyer** marched both of the Edict's prisoners to the dungeon on the Marshal's orders, and
felt rotten about it. He keeps a tally on the gatepost of everyone who's gone south and not come back:
eleven this year. **Elsa** keeps the house. **Will**, sixteen, has joined the bridge watch under Bran
Harrow. **Ruth**, twelve, wants to.

**Ties.** Serves the Harrows. Will and Bran. Brings Kat Linnet a basket every week, out of guilt.

**Rumours.**
- The eleven who went south are dead. **(false)** Seven are picking apples on the Fitzwaren manors with
  the Vael. Four are the cow Tam Appleby was looking for, and three of its friends.
- Tom Bowyer let a witch through the gate. **(true)** She said please.

### The Tansys

*Flower sellers.*

**Origins.** Orphaned by the Fever of 597 AF and raised by their grandmother on what the flowers bring
in.

**Today.** **Daisy**, fifteen, sells posies to the courtiers at the Fitzwarens' gate and hears everything.
**Pip**, ten, is a link-boy who carries a lantern for anyone walking home after dark, including a certain
princess. **Gran Tansy** grows the flowers in pots on every windowsill of the tenement.

**Ties.** Pip lights Rosamund's way. Daisy and the Fitzwarens' gossip. The day's last flower goes to the
Linnets.

**Rumours.**
- Pip Tansy knows the Princess. **(true)** She tips in pennies and pretends to be called Rose.
- Rowan's dearer than roses now. **(true)** Gran's started growing rowan.

## St Mary's

### Dean Anselm Thorne

*Dean of St Mary's.*

**Origins.** Third son of a southern lord. He's been Dean for twenty years, crowned Osric, and will not
see him called a liar.

**Today.** Preaches the Founder's Victory every autumn and has ordered iron grilles for every window of
St Mary's (paid for, secretly, by the Queen). Keeps the treasury key round his neck. Knows the gilded
"altar stones" are warm, and that the Ranger's orb beside them is cold, and has decided that's something
to pray about rather than think about.

**Ties.** Rival of Father Gale. Paid by the Queen. Blesses the bridge every spring.

**Rumours.**
- The Dean sleeps in the treasury. **(true)** On a cot, with a candle, next to the eggs.
- The Dean believes in dragons. **(half true)** He believes in one, and he's praying very hard that it
  doesn't believe in him.

### The Pryces

*Librarians of St Mary's since 211 AF.*

**Origins.** Queen Ethelind founded the line to look after St Mary's library, and swore the first Pryce to
secrecy on the fountain. Every Pryce since has been a priest and a librarian, and every one has known
there was a false shelf. Elias was the first in two centuries to look behind it.

**Today.** **Father Elias Pryce**, seventy-one, found the chronicle in 570 AF and took it to the King in
575 AF. He's been sorry ever since, and has hidden a copy where the Fells will find it if he dies.
**Mistress Joan Pryce**, his sister, keeps house and has read the copy, twice.

**Ties.** Shares the chronicle's secret with the King and Hugh Kellet. The copy is for the Fells. Old
friends with Widow Crane.

**Rumours.**
- There's a forbidden book in St Mary's library. **(true)**
- Father Pryce reads a prayer for the witches on Founder's Night. **(true)**

### The Gales

*Treasurers of St Mary's.*

**Origins.** A worldly family of churchmen and merchants. Father Matthias Gale's grandfather was a wool
merchant who bought him a good education and a better job.

**Today.** **Father Matthias Gale** counts the treasury's coins every night, twice, and would very much
like to be Dean. He tells the Marshal things to help it along. His two cats, **Tithe** and **Offering**,
sleep on the gilded altar stones because they're warm. He was the first to notice, and moved the candles
away. They stayed warm.

**Ties.** Informs for the Harrows. Rival of Dean Thorne. Hosts the clergy's Gilding Day dinner.

**Rumours.**
- Father Gale is the Marshal's man. **(true)**
- The treasury cats can see ghosts. **(half true)** They can hear the eggs. They purr back.

### The Kellets

*Master masons of St Mary's since Ulf Kellet laid its first stone.*

**Origins.** **Ulf Kellet** built St Mary's for Hereward the Mason in 88 AF, carved the first mason's
mark into its foundation stone, and was the first to notice that the oldest stones under the crypt hum.
Every Kellet since has been a mason on St Mary's, and every one has put a mark on a stone near that door.

**Today.** **Master Hugh Kellet**, fifty-nine, has finished the crossing spire, the tallest in the
kingdom. Under the crypt there's a small door, older than the church, carved with three flames, a
snowflake and a lightning bolt. Hugh has sealed it twice, and both times it was open by morning. After the
second time he went to Father Pryce, and they talked all night. **Mary** keeps the masons fed. **Ralph**,
their son, a widower, carves the new gargoyles as robed mages with fire in their hands. **Isabel**,
eight, explores the crypt with Tibby Waxley.

**Ties.** Knows the chronicle (through Pryce). Isabel and Tibby. The Waxleys supply the crypt candles.

**Rumours.**
- The gargoyles are a message to the witches. **(true)** "We remember you."
- Something lives under St Mary's. **(true)**

### The Waxleys

*Chandlers to St Mary's.*

**Origins.** Candle-makers to St Mary's since Hereward's day, with bees outside the walls for the wax.

**Today.** **Agatha Waxley** makes every candle St Mary's burns, and has sold three times the usual
number since the Edict. **Piers**, her husband, keeps the bees, which have started building their combs
in three colours: red, white and indigo. **Tibby**, twelve, explores the crypt with Isabel Kellet.

**Ties.** Supply St Mary's and the Kellets. Sell beeswax to Old Mother Larke for salves. Tibby and
Isabel.

**Rumours.**
- The Waxleys' bees make magic honey. **(half true)** The red honey's warm, the white honey's cold and
  the indigo honey fizzes. Nobody's brave enough to eat the indigo.

## The almshouse

### The Widow Crane

*Almswoman, the oldest soul in Kingsholm.*

**Origins.** Born in 509 AF. Her grandmother **Gwenna** saw the Summer the Sky Went Pink as a little girl,
and a shadow longer than the river cross the Greyspines at dawn. Agnes Crane grew up on that story and
has outlived three husbands and nine children telling it.

**Today.** Sits at the almshouse door every evening, facing south. Plays cards with Dowager Queen Hild
every Thursday, and cheats. The almshouse lets her talk because nobody believes the very old. They
should.

**Ties.** Hild of the Hills. Old Mother Larke (Ivy's grandmother delivered Agnes's first baby). Father
Pryce brings her the chronicle's best bits to check against Gwenna's stories.

**Rumours.**
- Widow Crane cheats at cards. **(true)**
- Widow Crane has met the Old Mother. **(half true)** Once, in a dream, when she was seven. The Old
  Mother told her to eat her greens.
