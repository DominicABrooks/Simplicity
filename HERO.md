# The Foreigner

Who the hero is, where he came from, why he helps, how a little stone fellow in a bark wrap becomes strong
enough to beat the mages, and how the story ends. The world he walks into is in [LORE.md](LORE.md),
[HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md) and [FAMILIES.md](FAMILIES.md).

## What the city calls him

The people of Kingsholm call him **the Foreigner**, because he came up out of the Witchwood, where nobody
comes from. Guards call him "you there". Children call him "the little stone one". Old Mother Larke calls
him "dearie", like everyone else. The Vael, once they meet him, call him **Aelin**: *little Ael*.

He never tells anyone his name, because he can't. He has no mouth. Nobody in Aurenmark has one (they talk
through the dark in their faces), but the Foreigner's face makes no sound at all. That makes him **the only
person in Kingsholm who cannot break the Edict of the Quiet Tongue**. The Marshal finds this extremely
annoying.

## Where he really comes from

### The heart-chip

Everyone in the world is a Pebbling, a chip of the sleeping stone **Ael** that woke up (see
[HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md#the-waking-where-everyone-comes-from)). Their faces are little windows onto the
Hush, lit from the inside by a spark of Ael's fire, and the dark behind the lamp is where the Gloom
peeks in.

The Foreigner is different. In the spring of **598 AF**, the night the three eggs in St Mary's first
grew warm, Ael felt the seal under the valley start to slip. Ael is asleep, and can't get up to fix it. So
it did what a sleeper does when something's wrong: it turned over, and a tiny chip came loose from the
stone closest to its heart and rolled up into the world.

That chip is the Foreigner. His face is a window like everyone's, but **it doesn't open onto the Hush.
It opens straight into Ael's heart.** His eyes aren't lit by a spark. They're lit by the heart-fire
itself, from the other side.

This means:

- **He can't go grey.** There's no Hush behind his face for the Gloom to look through. Every mage who
  draws magic risks greying; he never will.
- **The Gloom can't see him.** Umbrall looks at the world through cracks and through greyed eyes, and the
  Foreigner is a blank spot in all of them. That terrifies the Gloom, and it's why every greyed mage in
  the Witchwood attacks him on sight.
- **He has all three fires in him**, a little of each: Ember's warmth, Frost's stillness, Storm's
  get-up-and-go. Not enough to cast a spell, not yet, but enough to pick up any weapon and learn it.
- **He looks like the mages.** The Vael lived nearest Ael's heart for thousands of years, and their
  stone is the same stone he's chipped from. The bestiary isn't wrong: a mage *is* "a robed twin of the
  wanderer".
- **He can't be kept a sheep.** An Ember orb can rewrite a Pebbling's shape, but the Foreigner's shape
  is Ael's own first idea of a person. It snaps back in a few seconds, every time, looking slightly
  offended.

### The clearing

He wakes in a forest clearing in the Witchwood, on a flat old stone by a campfire, with nothing on but a
bark wrap. It's the same stone where, six hundred years ago, **Yssa of the Frost** left a bowl of milk for
a lost Plain Folk child, and where Queen Matilda ordered a bowl left every new moon after her. It's the
oldest stone in the valley and the nearest to Ael's heart.

The camp isn't his. It belongs to **Wystan Fell**, the King's Ranger, who camped here in 599 AF on his
way south, and again on his way home. He left in a hurry the second time. Under the stone the Foreigner
finds the Ranger's spare things, and a note in a careful hand:

> *If anyone finds this, and you're not a mage: go north through the cave to Kingsholm. Find Meg Fell in
> the Wallside, the house with the grey cloak at the window. Tell her the Vael were kind. Tell her I'm
> sorry. And tell someone, anyone, that the eggs have to go home.*

The Foreigner can't tell anyone anything, of course. But he can carry a note.

### Falling, and waking again

When the Foreigner falls in battle, he doesn't die the way other people do. His heart-fire goes back down
into Ael, and Ael, still asleep, turns over again and wakes him on the same stone by the same fire, in the
same bark wrap, with nothing. He doesn't remember much of what happened. But some of it stays in his toes.

That's why every run starts at the camp with nothing, and why he keeps coming back. Ael isn't going to stop
sending him until the seal is safe.

## Why he helps

He doesn't know any of the above when he wakes. He's a few seconds old and has no memory. Here's what
pulls him north anyway.

1. **The song.** From the moment he wakes he can feel the Old Mother's song in his toes, a deep slow purr
   in the ground. In the Witchwood it's faint and sad. It gets louder the further north he goes, and
   loudest of all in the crypt of St Mary's, where it sounds like crying. He follows it the way you'd
   follow someone calling your name.
2. **The wrong places.** Near a crack in the world, he feels *wrong*: cold in the middle, like a door left
   open. The Witchwood is full of them, and they're getting bigger. He doesn't know what they are, only
   that someone should close them, and nobody else seems to notice.
3. **The note.** The Ranger asked. He's going to deliver it.
4. **The people.** The first person in Kingsholm to be kind to him is **Wren Fell**, nine, who gives him a
   crust from her pocket and asks if he's seen her father. After that there's Meg at the window, Old
   Mother Larke squinting into his eyes and laughing, Pip Tansy lighting his way home, Joan Hollin's
   three-cut loaf, the Applebys' sheep who keeps staring at him like it knows something. He came out of the
   wood with nothing. The city gives him things, and he's the sort who pays back.
5. **What he's for.** Underneath all of it, Ael woke him for a reason. He was made to stand in the place
   where the dark gets in, and not let it.

## The mages he fights: the Greyed

The robed mages in the Witchwood aren't simply the Vael, and they aren't the Vael's army. They're
**the Greyed**: Vael wardens who were sent north to hold the cracks under the Witchwood shut while the
Vael decided what to do about Aurenmark. They held them for too long. Every spell opened their own windows
a little wider, and somewhere in the last two years they went grey: their robes faded, they forgot their
names, and now **Umbrall's eye looks out through theirs**.

The Greyed still look like Ember, Frost and Storm mages. They still throw fire, frost and lightning, blink
through the Hush, and draw swords when you catch them. But they don't knock any more, and they don't want
the eggs sent home. **They want the eggs to hatch where they are**, on top of the deepest crack in the
valley, because a hatching would break the seal wide open and let the Gloom straight up into the world.

When the Foreigner beats one, the mage doesn't bleed. The grey goes out of them all at once. Their robes
fall empty, and a little spark of real fire, the last of the person they used to be, flies off south toward
home. The Vael say those sparks go back into Ael, and wake up again somewhere, someday.

The **slimes** are the same story, smaller. Each one is a scrap of the people of Ul-Ael, stirred up by the
Gloom coming close. When he pops one, its little bit of somebody goes peacefully back to sleep.

### Their leader: Calder of the Storm

**Calder** was the captain of the wardens, the best Storm mage of his age and Yssa of the Frost's favourite
pupil. He held the biggest crack under the Witchwood alone for forty years. He went grey first, and
deepest. Now he leads the others, and Umbrall's eye has opened all the way behind his face.

Calder's plan is simple. On **Founder's Night**, the whole city gathers in the square to burn straw
witches (twice as many this year, by the Marshal's order) while saying the scary names out loud. Names
spoken in fear draw the Gloom like moths to a lamp. In all the smoke and shouting, Calder means to march
the Greyed over the King's Bridge, through Southgate and up East Street to St Mary's, and set the church
on fire with the eggs inside. A fire that hot would hatch them in minutes, right on top of the crack.

His lieutenants:

| Name | Order | What's left of them |
|---|---|---|
| **Cinder** | Ember | Once ran the Vael's bakeries. Still turns people into sheep, and still can't resist explaining why. |
| **Rime** | Frost | Once kept the Vael's oldest songs. Now hums the one note the Gloom hums, very beautifully. |
| **Calder** | Storm | Once the best of them. Calls lightning from a clear sky, and blinks so fast he seems to be in three places. |

## How he gets strong enough

He starts with nothing, can barely punch, and has five hearts. By the end he takes on a mage captain in a
burning church. Here's how, and how each piece of the game fits:

- **Levels are his heart-fire growing.** Every time he frees a greyed mage, some of the stolen fire passes
  through him on its way home, and a little of it stays. Every slime he pops is a bit of warmth returned.
  That's experience. Each level makes him a bit faster, every second level gives him another heart, and
  every fourth makes him hit harder, as the fire in him burns brighter. Up to **level 20**, where his eyes
  shine like a lantern.
- **Gear is the city arming him.** He gets swords, bows and armour from the Greyed he beats, from the
  Hammonds' forge (and in the end, Nell Hammond's cold sword), and from the Garths' storehouse when the
  Prince starts leaving doors unlocked. Every piece he wears shows on him, and the city can see the
  Foreigner getting grander every time he walks in.
- **Potions** are Old Mother Larke's tea, in bottles. Two hearts' worth, and slightly fizzy.
- **The dash and the jump** are Storm's get-up-and-go. **Swimming** is Frost: water doesn't scare him. The
  **fishing pole** came from the Ranger's camp, and the Rudds teach him what to do with it.
- **The crits**, the one-in-ten hits that land twice as hard, are the moments Ael's heart beats under the
  valley. Once a year for Ael, but a lot more often for anyone standing as close to it as he is.
- **He can't go grey and the Gloom can't see him.** That's the real reason he can win. The Greyed are
  stronger than he is for most of the game, but every one of them is fighting something its own eyes can't
  properly see.

## The road through the game

A suggested shape for the story, from the first step to the last.

### Act I: The Witchwood

He wakes on the old stone by the Ranger's fire. He finds the note, a fishing pole and a little bread. He
learns to punch on slimes and to fish in the lake. The first Greyed mage turns him into a sheep, he turns
back, and he chases it off. Somewhere at the edge of the trees, a tall woman in frost-white robes, with
eyes the right colour, watches him and doesn't come closer: **Yssa of the Frost**, who has been waiting
six hundred years for someone like him. The path north leads to the cave in the Greyspines.

### Act II: Kingsholm

Out of the cave, between the gilded lions, over the King's Bridge, through Southgate. The guards wave him
in, since he's clearly not a witch: he can't even say "witch". The city is gold and marble and frightened.

He delivers the Ranger's note to **Meg Fell**. After that, side stories open up across the city:

- **The falcon post.** Elinor Hawke's goshawk carries his answer to the Ranger's cell, and back comes
  Wystan's map of the Greyed camps.
- **Old Mother Larke** looks into his eyes and finds no crack at all. She laughs until she has to sit
  down, then teaches him about the cracks.
- **The sheep.** Beat Cinder, and Uncle turns back into **Tam Appleby**, who mostly wants a turnip.
- **The Crown's back room**, where Bethan Maddox decides he can sit in after curfew, and Princess
  Rosamund, in her laundress's hood, decides he's the most interesting person she's ever met.
- **The butchers' feud.** Help Hal Stott and Marian Cleave get their families to talk. The Stotts and the
  Cleaves then send the best of both halves of the shop to feed the defence.
- **The crypt.** Tibby Waxley and Isabel Kellet show him the little carved door under St Mary's, and
  Father Pryce gives him the copy of the true chronicle.
- **Prince Edmund**, who has been planning to ask his father for three months, finally does, with the
  Foreigner standing silently beside him.

### Act III: Founder's Night

The straw witches go up in the square. The Greyed come over the King's Bridge in the smoke, and the slimes
come up out of every pond in the Witchwood behind them. The bridge watch (Bran Harrow and young Will
Bowyer) holds the gate tower, Tom Bowyer won't shut Southgate on the people outside it, and the city, for
the first time in a year, shouts the true names of what's coming.

The Foreigner fights up the high street, across the square past the fountain king, and down East Street
to St Mary's, which is already burning. In the crypt, by the little carved door, he fights Cinder, then
Rime, then **Calder**, while the door behind them opens a crack and something enormous on the other side
opens its one eye.

## The end

When Calder falls, the grey goes out of him last of all. For a moment he's just a tired old Storm mage,
who looks at the Foreigner and says, in Vael, *"Oh. He sent you. Good."* Then his spark goes south, and
his robes fall empty on the crypt floor.

The eggs hatch anyway. They've been warm too long to stop. Three baby dragons tumble out, one red, one
white and one indigo, and the first thing they see is the Foreigner. They imprint on him on the spot.

Then the song changes. The three hatchlings start to sing, very small and very off-key, and the Old
Mother, arriving over Kingsholm at that moment, bigger than St Mary's and blotting out the moon, sings
with them. The whole city hears her, Edict or no Edict. The door under the crypt swings shut, and stays
shut, because now the seal is three living dragons who have decided they like it here.

**Afterwards:**

- **King Osric** stands at the market cross the next morning and reads out the true chronicle, all
  eleven pages of the Founder's letter included. He goes very red. The city decides to forgive him,
  mostly because the Old Mother is sitting on the city wall watching to see if they will.
- **The Edict is torn up.** Wystan Fell and Jack Linnet walk out of the dungeon. Jack sings the song with
  the real words at the cross, and Wystan goes home to the house with the grey cloak at the window.
- **The Vael come north**, openly, for the first time in six hundred years. Yssa of the Frost walks over
  the King's Bridge, sets a bowl of milk at the foot of the fountain, and sits down with Widow Crane and
  the Dowager Queen for a game of cards. Widow Crane cheats. Yssa lets her.
- **Founder's Night** becomes **the Night of the Return**. The straw witches go, and everyone puts out a
  bowl of milk instead.
- **The three hatchlings** stay in the valley as its new wardens, singing the seal shut. They follow the
  Foreigner everywhere they're allowed to, and several places they aren't.
- **The Foreigner** walks back down through the cave to the old stone in the Witchwood clearing, sits by
  the Ranger's fire, and looks up. Most of the stars are just stars again. One of them, very high up, is
  still getting bigger.

## Questions the story leaves for later

- **Is he Ael's only chip?** Ael turned over once in 598 AF. Who's to say nothing else rolled loose?
- **What does the heart-fire do at level 20 and beyond?** He has all three fires in him. One day he might
  cast.
- **Yssa's six hundred years.** She was there at the Founding. How is she still alive, and what has she
  been waiting for?
- **The star.** Umbrall lost a door. It will be looking for another.
