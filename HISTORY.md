# The History of the World

Where everything came from, why there are mages, what's lurking past the stars, and how Aurenmark got from
the very beginning to the King's very silly Edict. [LORE.md](LORE.md) covers the kingdom as it is now.

Most of this is known to almost nobody. The Vael remember it in songs, which are long and go on a bit.
St Mary's library holds some of it, in a book nobody is allowed to read. The rest is true whether anyone
remembers it or not.

## Before the world: the Hush

Before anything, there was the **Hush**: a big, quiet, endless dark with no up, no down and absolutely no
snacks.

The Hush isn't empty. Way out in it lives the **Gloom**, a hungry fog with far too many eyes, and at its
middle, bigger than anything, sleeps **Umbrall, the Hungry Dark**. Umbrall isn't evil exactly. It's just
*hungry*, and what it eats is colour, names and everything that makes one thing different from another.
Anything the Gloom nibbles turns grey and blank, forgets what it's called and wanders off humming one note.

Umbrall can't eat anything that's whole and happy. It gets in through cracks.

## Ael, the Great Sleeping Stone

Once, something wonderful came drifting through the Hush: **Ael, the Great Sleeping Stone**, a mountain
as big as a world, with three fires burning in its heart.

- **Ember**, its warmth: the fire that makes and changes things.
- **Frost**, its calm: the cold that keeps and remembers.
- **Storm**, its get-up-and-go: the lightning that decides and moves.

The Gloom chased Ael across the Hush for longer than there have been years, and in the end it caught up
and took a bite. Ael was cracked right through. It curled up tight around its three fires to keep them
safe, yawned an enormous yawn, and went to sleep.

**The world is Ael, still sleeping.**

- The **mountains** are its knobbly back. The Greyspines are its spine (that's why they're called that).
- The **gold** under the Greyspines is a little of its fire, gone solid and shiny. Aurenmark digs it up
  and sticks it on everything.
- The **river Aurel** starts at a crack in the Greyspines nobody's ever found the bottom of. If you put
  your ear to it, you can hear Ael snoring.
- The **sky** is Ael's warmth rising off it. Past the sky is the Hush, and the **stars** are the Gloom's
  eyes, peeking. Sailors who count the stars too long at sea swear some of them blink.

## The Waking: where everyone comes from

As Ael slept, little chips of its stone woke up. They were small, round and stone-grey, and each one had a
spark of Ael's fire inside. These were the first people, the **Pebblings**, and everyone since is one of
them: men, Vael, guards, mages, kings, all of them.

Every Pebbling has the same face, which nobody thinks about because everyone has one. It's a little patch
of night sky with **two glowing eyes** in it, lit by Ael's spark. The night in your face is a tiny window
onto the Hush, and your eyes are the lamps that keep the Gloom from peeking through. The Vael have a saying
for it: *a face is a window with a lamp in it, so keep your lamp lit.*

The Pebblings wrapped themselves in bark, because they were chilly and because it looked smart.

Every so often a Pebbling still wakes up today, somewhere old stone sits deep in the forest, blinking,
with no memory, no name and nothing on but a bark wrap. The Witchwood is said to be one of those places.

## The Old Mother and the first mages

Deep inside Ael, where its heart still beats once a year, the three fires hatched someone to look after
them: the **Old Mother**, the first and biggest dragon of all. She's Ember, Frost and Storm in one very
large shape, and she **sings**. You can't hear her song. You feel it in your toes, a deep purr in the
rock. While she sings, Ael's cracks stay shut and the Gloom can't get in.

The Pebblings who lived nearest the heart learned to feel the song. They became the **Vael**, the people
*of Ael*. They found that if a Pebbling listens hard enough, they can draw a thread of one of Ael's fires
*through the window in their own face*. That's what magic is. It's why a mage's eyes blaze when they cast,
and why spells come out of the face first, never the hands.

The Vael sorted the gift into three **orders**, one for each fire, and they're very proud of their
uniforms:

| Order | Robes | What they look after | What they do when you annoy them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ember** | Red | Ovens, forges, birthday candles | Fireballs, and a purple orb that turns you into a sheep (the easiest shape there is) |
| **Frost** | White | The songs, the history, the ice cream | Frostbolts that slow you down, and a nova that freezes everyone standing too close |
| **Storm** | Indigo and gold | Messages, weather, the mountain passes | Sparks faster than you can blink, and lightning out of a clear blue sky |

Every mage of every order can **blink**: pop out of the world and back in a few steps away. Where do you go
in between? Into the Hush, for a split second, through your own face. Mages are taught to hold their
breath and not look around. A few always peek.

### Don't overdo it

Every spell opens a mage's window a little wider, and the Gloom likes a wide window. A mage who casts too
much, too often, starts **going grey**: first their robes fade, then they forget little things, and then
their own name. Their eyes start to glow a colour that isn't Ael's. The orders send greying mages on long
holidays by the sea, which usually fixes it.

## The Jelly Night

Long ago, the Vael built their grandest city right down against Ael's heart, where the fires were
strongest: **Ul-Ael**, the Deep Hall. Its mages were the best ever, and also the worst at not overdoing
it. One night they cast so much that the floor of the city cracked open, and the Gloom came pouring up.

The Old Mother sang louder than she ever had and pushed the Gloom back down. She was just in time to save
everyone, and just too late to save them *properly*. When the dust settled, every single person in Ul-Ael
had been turned into **jelly**.

They're still around. In Aurenmark they're called **slimes**. They hop at anyone who comes near because
they're trying to say hello, and they're terrible at it. They're never alone because they're a whole city
that got scrambled together. Somewhere in all that jelly is a mayor, and the Frost order is still working
on turning them back.

To make sure it never happened again, the Vael built a temple on the hill above the biggest crack in the
valley. In the deep places under it, the Old Mother laid **three eggs**, one for each fire. Together the
eggs make a seal: a song you can hold. As long as the eggs sat under the hill, the crack stayed shut.

Then the Old Mother went off under the Greyspines for a nap. It's been a long nap.

## The Plain Folk

Some Pebblings didn't fancy magic. They'd seen what happened to Ul-Ael, or they couldn't hear the song,
or they just liked gardening better. They went down from the mountains to the river lands, built in
timber, grew turnips, forgot they were made of stone and called themselves **men**. Their eyes glow a
little dimmer than the Vael's. They think that's just how eyes are.

For a very long time men and Vael kept to themselves. Men told spooky stories about witches in the woods.
The Vael told spooky stories about men, mostly about the turnips.

## The ages, as far as anyone can count them

Years are counted from the Founding (*AF*). Before that, everyone counted differently and argued about it.

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| Before time | The Hush, the Gloom, and Umbrall at the middle of it, snoring. |
| The Bite | The Gloom catches Ael and bites it. Ael curls up and falls asleep, and becomes the world. |
| The Waking | The first Pebblings blink their eyes open. |
| The Singing | The Old Mother hatches from Ael's heart. The Vael learn her song, and the three orders form (and immediately argue about robe colours). |
| The Jelly Night | Ul-Ael overdoes it. Everyone in the Deep Hall becomes a slime. |
| The Sealing | The temple on the hill and the three eggs. The Old Mother goes for her nap. |
| The Parting | The Plain Folk go down to the rivers, become men and discover turnips. |
| ~40 before AF | The Great Turnip Blight in the south. Bands of men come north looking for somewhere new. |
| **0 AF** | **The Founding.** Aldric, a cheerful young man with a very large hat, wanders into the valley. The Vael feed him, give him a room and show him where the gold is. |
| 9 AF | **The Great Pinch.** Aldric throws the Vael the biggest party the valley has ever seen. While they're all dancing, he sneaks into the deep places, pinches the three eggs and the gold mines, and locks the temple door from the outside. The Vael, being very polite, leave him a strongly worded letter and move south over the Greyspines in a huff. Aldric names the forest the Witchwood and tells everyone he won a terrible battle. |
| 9–30 AF | **The Wobbly Years.** With nobody looking after the eggs, the valley goes a bit odd: the fog runs uphill, cats walk backwards, and it snows upwards twice. |
| 31 AF | Aldric I retires to the seaside. The letter is found framed in his bedroom. |
| 88 AF | **Hereward the Mason** builds St Mary's on the old temple. He finds the eggs, decides they're lovely, has them gilded and puts them in the treasury as "the stones of the first altar". The valley stops being odd. Hereward takes the credit. |
| 140–190 AF | **The Gold Kings.** The mines go deeper and deeper. In 186 AF a crew digs straight into what turns out to be the Old Mother's nostril. She sneezes, the whole shaft comes out of the mountain like a cork, and the crown seals it and pretends it was an earthquake. |
| 211 AF | **Queen Ethelind the Wise** reads the true chronicle, goes very red, hides it behind a false shelf in St Mary's library, and writes a much more flattering one that everyone learns today. |
| 260–274 AF | **The Salt War** with the coast kingdoms over who has the saltiest fish. The Harrows hold the old timber bridge for three days with a pushcart and a lot of shouting, and are made lords for it. |
| ~445 AF | **The Summer the Sky Went Pink.** The Old Mother rolls over in her sleep. All summer the sky glows pink at noon, it thunders on sunny days, and the river freezes in August. Widow Crane's grandmother is a little girl that year and never stops talking about it. The crown says it was a comet. |
| 446–550 AF | **The Long Snooze.** Nothing much happens. Founder's Night turns from a solemn service into a party with straw witches, which slowly become a children's game. |
| 553 AF | Osric IV is crowned, and **the Gilding** begins: marble, gold leaf, lions. In 560 AF he starts building the King's Bridge out of marble, paid for on credit. |
| 598 AF | The eggs in St Mary's start, very slightly, to get warm. Only the treasurer notices, and he assumes it's the candles. |
| 599 AF | Wystan Fell, the King's Ranger, goes south over the Greyspines and meets the Vael. They ask after their eggs. |
| **600 AF** | **Now.** Robed mages in the Witchwood, more slimes than anyone's ever seen, and the Edict of the Quiet Tongue. |

## What's really going on

Everyone in Kingsholm is wondering whether the witches are coming. That isn't the real problem.

**The Vael aren't coming for revenge.** Well, maybe a little bit, about the party. Mostly they're coming
because **the eggs are about to hatch**. Six hundred years in a warm treasury has done what six hundred
years usually does to eggs. When three baby dragons hatch right on top of the biggest crack in the valley,
the seal hatches with them, the crack opens, and the Gloom gets a straight path up into the world.

Out past the stars, one of Umbrall's enormous eyes has started to open.

The Ember, Frost and Storm mages in the Witchwood are Vael wardens, sent ahead to keep the cracks under
the forest shut while the grown-ups decide what to do about Aurenmark. That's why the slimes are so
stirred up: the Gloom is getting close. The mages would really rather just knock on the door of St Mary's
and ask for the eggs back politely. They're not sure Aurenmark is a polite sort of place.

The Old Mother is waking up too. She can feel her eggs getting warm, and she's coming to collect them.
She's very big and very grumpy before breakfast.

### The silly Edict that isn't silly

The Vael have one rule older than the orders: **don't say the name of what you're scared of out loud. The
Gloom can hear it.** Names spoken in fear draw it in, like moths to a lamp. King Osric banned scary talk to
save face. By complete accident, it's the single most sensible thing he's ever done. The Vael think this
is the funniest thing they've ever heard.

### What the chronicle doesn't say

The true chronicle tells of the party, the pinch and the polite letter. It doesn't say why Aldric's men
wouldn't go near the temple for a week afterwards. The Frost order remembers. Under the temple floor,
right where the biggest crack ran, something in the dark had opened one eye, looked up at the valley and
**smiled**.

The little door under St Mary's crypt that the Kellets keep sealing, the one carved with three flames, a
snowflake and a lightning bolt, isn't the Vael's door. It's the Vael's lock on somebody else's door, and
lately it keeps coming unlocked.

## Loose threads

Things the story leaves open for later:

- **The wanderer.** A little Pebbling who wakes up in the Witchwood with nothing but a bark wrap, and who
  the mages look an awful lot like. Newly woken? A mage who went grey and came back? Something Ael sent on
  purpose?
- **The sheep.** An Ember mage's orb turns you into a sheep. Does Tam Appleby remember being Tam? (He
  certainly remembers liking turnips.)
- **Three baby dragons.** Who looks after them when they hatch? They'll imprint on the first thing they see.
- **The slime mayor.** If the Frost order ever unscrambles Ul-Ael, it'll want its city back.
- **The stars.** Some of them blink. One of them has been getting bigger.
- **Ael.** It's asleep, not gone. What happens to the world when it wakes up?
