# The History of the World

Where everything came from, why there are mages, what lives outside the world, and how Aurenmark got from
the beginning to the Edict of the Quiet Tongue. [LORE.md](LORE.md) covers the kingdom as it is now.

Most of this is known to almost nobody. The Vael remember some of it in song. St Mary's chronicle holds a
little more, in pieces. The rest is true whether anyone remembers it or not.

## Before the world: the Hush

Before anything, there was the **Hush**: a dark that goes on forever in every direction, with no up, no
time and no sound.

The Hush isn't empty. Things live in it. The Vael call them the **Unsung**, because no song has a name for
them and no song should. They're vast and have no shape. They don't think the way anything with a shape
thinks. They aren't evil, any more than cold is. They're hungry, and what they eat is *meaning*: shape,
name, warmth, memory, the difference between one thing and another. Something the Unsung have eaten isn't
dead. It's just *undone*, and goes on moving without anyone inside it.

The Unsung can't get into anything whole. They get in through cracks.

## The fall of Ael

Something came drifting through the Hush, from somewhere else or from before. The Vael call it **Ael, the
Firstborn Stone**. It was a living mountain with three fires burning in its heart:

- **Ember**, its heat: the fire that makes and changes things.
- **Frost**, its stillness: the cold that keeps and remembers.
- **Storm**, its will: the lightning that decides and moves.

Ael was fleeing. The Unsung had eaten everything else it knew, and they followed it. Somewhere in the Hush
they caught it and tore into it, and Ael was cracked through. It folded itself shut around its wounds and
its three fires, and went to sleep.

**The world is Ael's sleeping body.**

- The **mountains** are its bones. The Greyspines are its spine.
- The **gold** under the Greyspines is its blood, run cold. Aurenmark spends it on gilding.
- The **rivers** are the thaw running off it. The Aurel rises from a crack in the Greyspines no one has
  ever found the bottom of.
- The **sky** is the last warmth coming off it, and above the sky is the Hush. The stars are the places
  where the Unsung press closest. Nobody in Aurenmark knows this. Sailors who stare at the stars too long
  at sea come home quieter.

## The Waking: where people come from

As Ael slept, chips of its stone woke up. They were small, round and stone-grey, each with a little of
Ael's fire in it. These were the first people, the **Wakened**, and every person since is one of them.

Every Wakened face has the same mark, which nobody thinks about because everyone has it. Where a face
should be there is a **dark opening**, and in it two eyes glowing with Ael's fire. The dark in your face
is a crack. It goes all the way down to the Hush, and the light in your eyes is the only thing standing in
it. When a person dies, their eyes go out and the crack is just dark.

The old Vael saying is that *a face is a door with a lamp in it.*

The Wakened learned to wrap themselves in bark and cloth. Some say that was because they were cold. The
Vael say it was so they wouldn't have to see each other's faces too much.

Some Wakened still wake now. Here and there, where old stone sits deep in the forest, someone opens their
eyes with no memory, no name and nothing but a bark wrap. The Witchwood is said to be one of those places.

## The Old Mother and the first mages

Deep in Ael's body, where its heart still beat slowly, the three fires gave birth to a creature to keep
them: the **Old Mother**, the first and greatest dragon. She is Ember, Frost and Storm in one shape, and
she **sings**. Her song is too low to hear and is felt in the stone instead. While she sings, the cracks in
Ael's body stay closed.

The Wakened who lived nearest the heart learned to hear her. They became the **Vael**, the people *of
Ael*. They found that a Wakened who listens can draw a thread of one of Ael's fires *through the crack in
their own face*. That's what magic is. It's why a mage's eyes blaze when they cast, and why a spell always
comes out of the face end first, never the hands.

The Vael organised the gift into three **orders**, one for each fire:

| Order | Fire | What it keeps | What it does in a fight |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ember** | Heat, change | The forges, the hearths, the art of making | Fire, and the purple orb that rewrites a body's shape (a sheep is the easiest shape there is) |
| **Frost** | Stillness, memory | The songs, the records and the dead | Frostbolts that slow, and the nova that stops everything nearby |
| **Storm** | Will, movement | The messengers and the wardens of the passes | Sparks faster than the eye, and lightning called down from a clear sky |

Every mage of every order can **blink**, stepping out of the world and back in a little way off. Nobody
says aloud where you go in between. You go into the Hush, for a moment, through your own face. Most mages
who blink often start to hear something there. The orders train it out of them, mostly.

### The price

Every spell opens the caster's crack a little, and the Hush looks through. A mage who draws too much, too
often, starts to lose things: first names, then faces, then the difference between one thing and another.
The orders call it **going quiet**. A mage who goes all the way quiet is still walking and casting, but
nobody is home, and their eyes are lit by something that isn't Ael's fire.

## The First Dark

Long before men, the Vael built their greatest city deep inside Ael, right against the heart, where the
fires were strongest: **Ul-Ael**, the Deep Hall. Its mages drew more than anyone ever had, and the cracks
under the city opened.

Nobody knows exactly what happened, because nobody came out to tell it. The Frost order's oldest songs say
the city went silent in one night, and its lamps stayed lit, the wrong colour, for a hundred years. Things
came up out of the deep after that: soft, shapeless and hungry for warmth, hopping at anything that
moved. They were what was left when the Unsung had finished with Ul-Ael's people. They're still around.
In Aurenmark they're called **slimes**. They're never alone, because they were never separate to begin
with: every slime is a scrap of one undone city.

To close the wound, the Old Mother sang louder than she ever had. The Vael carried her song up to the
surface and built a temple on the hill above the deepest crack in the valley. In the deep places under it, the Old
Mother laid **three eggs**, one for each fire. They're the keystones of a seal, a song made solid. As long as the eggs
lay under the hill and the Vael tended them, the cracks under the valley stayed shut.

Then the Old Mother went down under the Greyspines and slept, to keep singing in her sleep. She's been
asleep ever since, mostly.

## The Plain Folk

Not all the Wakened wanted to listen. Some had seen what drawing on the fires did to the Deep Hall, and
some just didn't hear the song. They went down from the mountains to the river lands, farmed, built in
timber, forgot they were made of stone, and called themselves **men**. Their eyes burn a little dimmer than
the Vael's. They think that's just how eyes are.

For thousands of years men and Vael lived apart. Men told stories about witches in the woods, and the
Vael didn't think of men much at all.

## The ages, as far as anyone can count them

Years are counted from the Founding (*AF*). Before it, nobody counted in a way that matches up.

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| Before time | The Hush, and the Unsung in it. |
| The fall | Ael is caught, cracked and falls asleep. Its body becomes the world. |
| The Waking | The first Wakened open their eyes. |
| The Singing | The Old Mother is born from Ael's heart. The Vael learn to hear her, and the three orders form. |
| The First Dark | Ul-Ael, the Deep Hall, is undone in a night. Slimes come up out of the deep. |
| The Sealing | The temple on the hill and the three eggs. The Old Mother goes under the Greyspines to sleep. |
| The Parting | The Plain Folk go down to the rivers and become men. |
| ~1000 before AF | Men's first kingdoms rise and fall along the coast. None of them come near the valley. |
| ~40 before AF | A hard famine in the south. Bands of men come north looking for land. |
| **0 AF** | **The Founding.** Aldric comes to the valley as a beggar. The Vael feed him, give him land and show him where the gold lies. |
| 9 AF | Aldric burns the temple with the Vael inside, takes the three eggs and the mines, and drives the Vael south over the Greyspines. The kingdom of Aurenmark begins. |
| 9–30 AF | **The Thaw Years.** Without the Vael tending the eggs, the seal goes slack. The valley has odd summers and slow-moving fogs, and children are born who don't cry. Aldric orders the forest south of the valley called the Witchwood and forbids anyone to enter it. |
| 31 AF | Aldric dies, raving that his face is "letting the cold in". The chronicle notes his eyes had gone out three days before he stopped breathing. |
| 88 AF | **Hereward the Mason** builds St Mary's on the temple's foundations. He has the eggs gilded and kept in the new treasury, where pilgrims are shown them as "the stones of the first altar". The odd summers stop. Hereward never says whether he knew why. |
| 140–190 AF | **The Gold Kings.** The mines are worked deep, too deep. Miners report singing in the rock. In 186 AF the deepest shaft is sealed after a whole crew comes up with their eyes lit the wrong colour. |
| 211 AF | **Queen Ethelind the Wise** reads the Founder's chronicle, hides it behind a false shelf in St Mary's library, and writes the official history everyone learns today. |
| 260–274 AF | **The Salt War** with the coast kingdoms over the river trade. The Harrows hold the bridge (the old timber bridge) and are ennobled for it. |
| ~445 AF | **The Summer the Sky Burned.** The Old Mother turns over in her sleep. For one summer the sky is red at noon, lightning falls from clear skies, and the river freezes in the August heat. Widow Crane's grandmother is a girl that year. The crown calls it a comet. |
| 446–520 AF | **The long quiet.** Founder's Night grows from a mass into a bonfire, and the straw witches become a children's game. |
| 553 AF | Osric IV is crowned. **The Gilding** begins: marble, gold leaf, lions. In 560 AF he starts the King's Bridge in marble, borrowing against the southern road. |
| 598 AF | The eggs in St Mary's begin, very slightly, to grow warm. Nobody notices but the treasurer. |
| 599 AF | Wystan Fell, the King's Ranger, goes south over the Greyspines and finds the Vael. |
| **600 AF** | **Now.** Robed figures in the Witchwood, slimes coming up thicker than anyone remembers, and the Edict of the Quiet Tongue. |

## What's really happening now

Everyone in Kingsholm thinks the question is whether the witches are coming. That isn't the question.

**The Vael aren't coming back for revenge, or not only for that.** Six hundred years without the eggs in
their place have worn the seal under the valley thin. The slimes in the Witchwood are multiplying because
the cracks under it are opening. The Old Mother is waking because she can feel it, and she's coming for
her eggs because they are about to hatch, wherever they are. A dragon hatching from a keystone, in a
gilded treasury, on top of the deepest crack in the valley, would break the seal completely.

The Ember, Frost and Storm mages in the Witchwood are the Vael's first wardens, sent to hold the cracks
under the forest shut while they decide what to do about Aurenmark. They'd rather not have to burn their
way to St Mary's to get the eggs back. They will if they must, because they remember Ul-Ael and Aurenmark
doesn't.

### The Edict, again

The Vael have one rule older than the orders: **don't say the name of what you fear aloud. It hears.**
The Unsung are drawn to names spoken in fear the way moths come to a lamp. Osric made fearful talk a
hanging matter to protect his crown. Without knowing it, he made the one rule that might be slowing the
dark down. The Vael find this very funny, in a grim way.

### What Father Pryce's chronicle doesn't say

The chronicle tells of the eggs, the temple and the burning. It doesn't say what the Vael found in the
burned temple afterwards, which is why Aldric's men fled the hill for a week. The Frost order remembers.
In the temple's undercroft, where the deepest crack ran, the floor had come open in the heat. Something
under it had **noticed** the valley for the first time in an age, and spent the six hundred years since
waiting for the song to stop.

The door under St Mary's crypt that the Kellets keep sealing, carved with three flames, a snowflake and a
lightning bolt, isn't the Vael's door. It's the Vael's lock, on something else's door, and it isn't
locked any more.

## Open threads

Things the history deliberately leaves unanswered, for later:

- **The wanderer.** A stone spirit who wakes in the Witchwood with nothing but a bark wrap, and who the
  mages look so much like. Is it newly Wakened, a Vael gone quiet and come back, or something Ael sent?
- **The sheep.** An Ember mage's orb rewrites a body's shape. Does Tam Appleby remember being Tam?
- **The Old Mother's side.** She's Ael's heart-warden, not the Vael's pet. If the seal can't be saved, she
  might decide the valley has to be burned clean, men and Vael with it.
- **The stars.** The sailors who come home quieter. Something up there is closer than it was.
- **Ael itself.** It's asleep, not dead. Nobody, not even the Vael, knows what happens to the world if it
  wakes up.
